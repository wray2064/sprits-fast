# Getting started

Five minutes from an empty canvas to a sprite you can use.

## 1. Start

When the program opens it shows the **start screen**.

- On the left, the ways in: **Open...**, **Import sprite sheet...**, the **Library** and this
  **User guide**.
- In the middle, a card for each preset size, and under them the files you opened recently,
  each with a picture of its own (the first time, there are three ways into this guide instead).
- On the right, the details of the new document (name, size, background and the starting
  palette) and a tip below.

Pick a preset card (**Tile 32 x 32** is a good first one) and press **Create**, or choose
**Start with a blank canvas**. For a canvas of your own size, set the width and height on the
right. **File > New** (`Ctrl+N`) brings the start screen up for the same questions.

> You can bring the start screen back any time with **File > Start screen**.

## 2. Draw

1. The pencil is chosen already. Press `B` for it any time.
2. Click a colour in the **Palette** panel on the left, then click and drag on the canvas.
3. Press `E` for the eraser, `G` to fill an area, `I` to pick a colour from the canvas.
4. Press `R` for a rectangle, `U` for an ellipse, `L` for a line. Drag them out, then drag
   their handles: a shape stays a shape.
5. Scroll the wheel to zoom, hold `Space` and drag to pan, `Ctrl+0` to fit the canvas to the
   window.

Made a mistake? `Ctrl+Z` undoes, `Ctrl+Shift+Z` (or `Ctrl+Y`) redoes, as far back as the
undo depth in Preferences allows.

## 3. Use layers

The **Layers** panel on the right lists the layers. **Add** makes a new one above the
picked one. A layer holds elements, one per colour you painted in plus any shapes; the
**Elements** panel lists them. Everything is described in [Layers and elements](topic:layers).

## 4. Make it move (optional)

Press `T` to open the timeline. **+ Frame** adds a frame; draw on it; press `Enter` to
play. The timeline opens over the canvas when you want it and tucks away when you do not;
**Pin** keeps it docked. See [Animation](topic:animation).

## 5. Save

**File > Save** (`Ctrl+S`) asks where to put a `.lsprite` file, which keeps everything:
layers, frames, palettes, shapes and history of what each element is.

## 6. Get it out

For use in a game or on the web, **File > Export PNG** writes a picture at 1x to 16x,
**Export animation** writes a GIF, animated PNG or WebP, and **Export sheet** lays the
frames into one image with a description a game engine can read. After the first export,
**File > Export again** (`Ctrl+Shift+E`) repeats it to the same file with the current
settings. See [Opening, saving and exporting](topic:files).
